Index of Bessarabian parish registers (ANRM fond 211)

Fond 211 of the National Archives of the Republic of Moldova (ANRM) holds the parish registers of Bessarabia (births, marriages, deaths), 1810-1931. FamilySearch (collection “Moldova, Church Books, 1811-1936”) has filmed all of them, but a village can only be found if you already know the file number and the film. This page gives, for each village in each file, its years, its church and the link to the reel, opened at the village's page when it is known.

What a parish register is

Under Russian rule (1812-1918), then under Romanian rule, Bessarabia had no civil registry: births, marriages and deaths were recorded by the churches. Each parish kept a parish register (метрическая книга, metricheskaya kniga, “metrical book”), one book per year, in two copies: one stayed at the church, the other was sent to the diocese. Fond 211 of the National Archives holds the copies that survived.

A register has three parts: the first for births and baptisms, the second for marriages, the third for deaths. Each part numbers its acts from 1. From the 1830s the book is a printed form in Russian, divided into columns, which the priest fills in by hand, in Russian or, in the early years, in Romanian written in Cyrillic. An act is read column by column, as in the example below.

A double page from the births part of the register of Tomai (uezd of Izmail) for 1882 (ANRM 211-14/33, page 3 of the file): four baptisms of girls, numbers 3 to 6, in January. Hover a marker to see it on the page.

The double page

1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9
  1. 1Printed heading: “Метрической книги на 1882 годъ, часть первая, о родившихся” (Metricheskoy knigi na 1882 god, chast pervaya, o rodivshikhsya): “Of the metrical book for the year 1882, part one, on those born”. The heading runs across the two pages: the year on the left, the rest on the right. The first part is the births.
  2. 2Act number: “Счётъ родившихся” (Schyot rodivshikhsya), the count of those born, in two sub-columns, one for boys and one for girls; the second is headed “женска пола” (zhenska pola), female sex. Each sex is counted separately. The four acts of this page are girls, numbers 3 to 6.
  3. 3Month and day: “Мѣсяцъ и день” (Mesyats i den), month and day, in two sub-columns: the day of birth, then the day of baptism. The month is written once, at the top of the column (“Генваря”, Genvarya, “of January”), and holds for the four acts.
  4. 4The child: “Имена родившихся” (Imena rodivshikhsya), the names of those born: a given name only, no surname.
  5. 5The parents: “Званіе, имя, отчество и фамилія родителей, и какого вѣроисповѣданія” (Zvanie, imya, otchestvo i familiya roditeley, i kakogo veroispovedaniya): rank, given name, patronymic and surname of the parents, and their confession. The text always follows the same order: rank and village, the father, “законная жена его” (zakonnaya zhena ego), “his lawful wife”, the mother, then the confession and whether it is a first marriage.
  6. 6The godparents: “Званіе, имя, отчество и фамилія воспріемниковъ” (Zvanie, imya, otchestvo i familiya vospriemnikov): rank, given name, patronymic and surname of the godparents. Act 4 names a single godparent, a man.
  7. 7Who baptised: “Кто совершалъ таинство крещенія” (Kto sovershal tainstvo kreshcheniya): who performed the sacrament of baptism, that is the priest and the psalm-reader. Their names are also written at the end of each act, on the left page.
  8. 8Witnesses’ signatures: “Рукоприкладство свидетелей” (Rukoprikladstvo svidetelei), the signatures of the witnesses. The column is empty here.
  9. 9Folio numbers: Added by hand, after the fact: 199 in red pencil at the top right, and a 3 in ink above it. Several numberings can coexist on a page: check which one the inventory follows.

  1. 1Act number: “4”, in the girls’ column: the fourth girl born in this part.
  2. 2Dates: 12 and 17: born on 12 January 1882, baptised on 17 January.
  3. 3The child: “Анна” (Anna): Anna.
  4. 4The parents: “Крестьянинъ с. Томай Измаильскаго уѣзда Бессарабской губерніи” (Krestyanin s. Tomai Izmailskago uyezda Bessarabskoy gubernii): “peasant of the village of Tomai, uezd of Izmail, government of Bessarabia”; then the father, “Иванъ Димитріевъ” (Ivan Dimitriev), Ivan son of Dimitrie, and his surname; then “законная жена его Елисавета Георгіева” (zakonnaya zhena ego Elisaveta Georgieva), “his lawful wife Elisaveta, daughter of Gheorghe”. The mother has no surname of her own here: the patronymic says whose daughter she is.
  5. 5Confession and marriage: “оба православнаго вѣроисповѣданія, первобрачные” (oba pravoslavnago veroispovedaniya, pervobrachnye): “both of the Orthodox confession, in their first marriage”.
  6. 6The clergy: “Священникъ … съ п. ч. псаломщикомъ …” (Svyashchennik … s p. ch. psalomshchikom …): “the priest … with the cleric, psalm-reader …”, followed by their names, written across the columns at the end of the act. “п. ч.” (p. ch.) stands for “причетникомъ” (prichetnikom), “cleric”.

How to use this page

  1. You need a FamilySearch account. It is free.
  2. Type the name of a village. The inventory writes it in Romanian (Tomai); the reel is filed under its Russian name of the time, in Latin letters. Both spellings are searched. Cyrillic input works too (Томай, Tomai). Accents do not matter, and the first letters of the name are enough. You can add a year (Tomai 1882) or a file number (14/33).
  3. Narrow the list by uezd (the district), by church and by years. The church list gives, in brackets, the number of lines of each church.
  4. Read a result. Each line is one village in one file. At the top: the file reference (211-14/33), the village as the inventory writes it and, in smaller type, the name the film is filed under, when it differs. Then the years, the uezd, the church and the folios of the village in the file.
  5. Click the button. “Open” opens the image on FamilySearch at the first page of the village, give or take a few images. When the village could not be located on the film, there is no button but a plain link: “start of the file” opens the first image of the file, or a title image just before it; when the start of the file is not known either, “files on the reel” opens the list of files on the reel, where you pick the file by its reference. If you find the right image, “Suggest a link” lets you send its link.
  6. Then turn the pages. Read the folio number written on the page, compare it with the folios of the result, and go forward or back.

Folios, and the counter of FamilySearch

A file is cited as 211-opis/file: 211-14/33 is fond 211, inventory (opis) 14, file 33. For each village the inventory gives its folios (filele, листы, listy): the leaves of the original book, numbered by the archive, for example “folios 39-75”.

A folio is not an image. An image of the reel usually shows a double page, that is two folios side by side, and the same leaf is sometimes photographed twice. The folios of the inventory therefore tell where a village sits in the file and how long it is, not an image number.

A reel of microfilm carries several files one after the other, and the FamilySearch viewer opens one file at a time. The counter it shows (“955 of 1,182”) is the counter of the file, not of the reel: the image is the right one even when the number looks small.

Village names

Villages are written in Romanian, with the spelling of the Archives' inventory (Năvârneţ); the current spelling (Nevîrneț) is given next to it. FamilySearch has catalogued the films under the Russian name of the time, transcribed in Latin letters. The search accepts both, and also older spellings of the same village (Berezloghi for Berezlogi). When the two names differ, the line of the result shows both.

Many villages share the same name, in different uezds, and some villages have several churches, each with its own register. The church (its hram, that is its patron saint or feast: Sf. Nicolae, Adormirea) and the uezd tell them apart. Check both before opening an image. Church names are given as the inventory writes them, in Romanian (Sf. Nicolae, Adormirea Maicii Domnului).

Traps

  • Years are missing. When a village has no line for a year, the register of that year may have been lost or never filmed, or the inventory may have been misread when this index was built. The index follows the inventory of the Archives, read by machine: a gap in the index does not prove that the register does not exist.
  • A village can be in the file of a neighbouring parish. A village without a church was written in the register of the parish it belonged to, often under the name of that parish. Search for the neighbouring villages too.
  • Dates follow the Julian calendar. The Russian Empire kept the old style: 12 days behind the Western calendar in the 19th century, 13 days in the 20th. A birth written on 12 January 1882 is 24 January in the Gregorian calendar.
  • Names are written the Russian way. Given names take their Russian form (Иванъ, Ivan, for Ioan; Георгій, Georgiy, for Gheorghe), and a person is first identified by a patronymic: Георгіева (Georgieva) means “daughter of Gheorghe”, it is not a surname. The surname, when written, comes after.
  • Links are accurate to a few images. The first page of a village is located on the film, not read page by page. Open it, read the folio written on the page, then go forward or back.
